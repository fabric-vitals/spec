# Fabric Vitals — Scenarios and scoring-formula comparison (v0.2)

**Status:** Fabric Vitals v0.2 (draft) — a technical proposal for discussion. Not a standard. Rulebook values not yet set.

This file supports two parts of the specification: how the vitals are combined into one score (aggregation), and the named conditions that put a ceiling on the score (critical conditions). See [`SPEC.md`](SPEC.md) §6 and §7. It records:
1. **the scoring-formula comparison** behind the choice of the weakest-vital rule, under which the score equals the lowest of the vitals (the minimum): how eight families of aggregation formula treat the masking example, a test case described below;
2. **the failure-scenario table**: for nineteen representative AI-fabric failures and events, which vitals should move, whether a critical condition should cap the Vitals Score (the headline number for the fabric), and on what time scale the underlying event recovers.

Every number in the comparison is ILLUSTRATIVE, meaning chosen only to show how a formula behaves, unless it cites a source. The scenario table is qualitative except where a source is cited. Three kinds of ID appear. Parameter IDs (T followed by a number) name Rulebook parameters, the settings that say how every fabric is measured and judged; they are listed in [`RULEBOOK.md`](RULEBOOK.md). Spec-sheet fields (S followed by a number) name what the fabric owner declared about the fabric; they are listed in [`SPEC_SHEET.md`](SPEC_SHEET.md). Metric IDs (M followed by a number) name the measured quantities listed in [`METRICS.md`](METRICS.md).

## 1. Scoring-formula comparison: the masking example

**The masking example.** This is the test case. It is called masking because a bad vital can be hidden, or masked, by good ones. Four vitals are healthy and one has collapsed:
- test vector, the five vital values fed to each formula: 100, 100, 100, 100, 10 (each on a scale of 0–100, higher is healthier);
- comparator, the fabric the collapsed one is compared with: a balanced fabric with all five vitals at 82;
- weights: ILLUSTRATIVE equal weights (1/5), because no source supplies weights for AI-fabric health;
- cap margin *m*, override threshold *T* and cap *C*, the constants used by the cap-based family below: ILLUSTRATIVE.

A formula passes the test if it does not score the collapsed fabric like the balanced one, that is, if the collapse shows in the score.

### Readings

- **M.3042's two-stage form (family 8)** is non-compensatory only *within* a vital, across entity types. It scores the masking example's collapse at 82.0, the same as family 1, because that collapse happens *across* vitals, and across vitals M.3042 adds rather than takes the minimum. It therefore fails the masking example and cannot be the recommended aggregator unless that test is dropped. Its inner rule is still a useful precedent: the Fabric Vitals rule within a vital already reports the worst entity, and M.3042 is the first ITU-T text found that aggregates by minimum inside a health index. See SPEC.md §6.
- **Only the arithmetic mean**, and anything algebraically equal to it for a collapse across vitals (weighted impairment subtraction, OWA with orness 0.5, and M.3042's two-stage form; orness measures how far the order weights lean towards the best or the worst values, and this value is the neutral midpoint), scores the collapsed network the same as a uniformly good one. That rules it out as the sole aggregator, as the OECD/JRC Handbook's own example of (21,1,1,1) against (6,6,6,6) anticipates (JRC is the European Commission's Joint Research Centre) [4]. It also means that M.3042's level-1 and level-2 weighted sums do not meet the masking example as written, even though M.3042 uses the minimum inside some level-3 indexes (family 8) [1].
- **The geometric mean**, the HDI's answer to the compensation problem [5], still returns 63 for a network whose congestion health is 10. Whether 63 is acceptable is a policy judgement, and because the result is so sensitive to the floor, the normalization floor becomes in effect a policy parameter.
- **Minimum, unweighted impairment subtraction and small-margin caps** all let the collapsed vital decide the score. The minimum is the only one of them that needs no weights and no cap constants.

## 2. Failure scenarios

For each scenario, the table gives:
- which vitals should change, the primary vital first and then the secondary ones it drags along (primary → secondary); the vital names are given in the table the first time each appears;
- the direction and size of the change, described in words unless a source gives a figure;
- whether a critical condition should cap the Vitals Score. A critical condition is one of six named states that put a ceiling on the score: CC1, a partition or black hole; CC2, a PFC storm or deadlock (PFC, priority flow control, is the pause signal of a lossless traffic class; a storm is when pause frames spread from switch to switch until traffic stalls); CC3, sustained loss on a lossless class; CC4, a single point of failure on capacity that carries jobs; CC5, redundancy exhausted; CC6, uncorrectable errors beyond the budget set by the IEEE (the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers, which publishes the Ethernet standards). See SPEC.md §7;
- how long the underlying event takes to recover, on the evidence of the cited sources.

The score's own hold time (how long a condition stays active after it clears) and decay time (how fast the score recovers afterwards) are Rulebook parameters T17 and T18, both VALUE NOT SET. The scenarios are also the basis of validation experiment E1, described in [`VALIDATION.md`](VALIDATION.md).

| # | Scenario | Dimensions that change (primary → secondary) | Direction and magnitude | Cap? | Recovery behaviour (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Link failure | D1 (reachability) → D2 (resilience), D6 (effective capacity) | Down. Small if the link was redundant and carried a small share of capacity: losing one T0–T1 link (a link between the first two switch tiers) is 3% of node capacity in the paper's hypothetical 800G-plane topology, by design arithmetic [22]. Large if the node was single-homed, that is, attached by one link only. | Yes if CC4 or CC5 applies, which is where the spec sheet promised the redundancy that has now been lost (fields S4 and S10); otherwise no | Bypassing the failed path in the transport takes a few tens of µs (microseconds; a design figure for MRC, the multipath transport of the cited paper); remapping the NIC port takes a few seconds [22]; BGP (Border Gateway Protocol, the routing protocol between the switches) takes from under a second to several seconds [23]; a job that crashed rolls back hours of work [24]. D2 stays low until the link is repaired. |
| 2 | Optic degradation (an optical transceiver, the module that drives the fibre, slowly failing) | D5 (physical link integrity) → D3 (congestion and loss, once packets are lost), D1 (if the link flaps) | Gradual decline in D5; D3 moves once loss appears. A fault in one direction only may not show as link-down [24]. | No, unless it escalates to CC1, CC3 or CC6 | Persists until the optic is replaced. Flaps last "several seconds" per event [25]. |
| 3 | Elevated BER (bit error ratio: the share of bits arriving in error on a link) | D5 → D3 | D5 falls as the margin to the IEEE budget shrinks. Errors that FEC (forward error correction, the check data that lets a receiver repair some bit errors) cannot correct raise the loss seen by D3. | Yes if CC6 | Certifying that a link meets a BER level needs a minimum observation window: ≥30 s for 10⁻¹² on 100GbE, ≥75 s for 10⁻¹³ on 400GbE (GbE is gigabit Ethernet), with no errors seen, at ~95% confidence. The window shrinks in proportion as the line rate rises. These are modelled figures from an IEEE 802.1 contribution on TSN (time-sensitive networking) fronthaul [26]. Recovery follows a repair. |
| 4 | ECMP imbalance (ECMP, equal-cost multipath, spreads flows across parallel paths of equal length by hashing; imbalance is when the hash sends too many flows down the same path) | D6 → D3, D4 (delay and tail) | D6 falls on fabrics that place each flow by hash (Meta saw up to >30% training degradation from flows pinned to the same path [27]). Not applicable to fabrics that spray packets across all paths. | No | Persists until the flows are re-hashed or traffic engineering moves them (the Meta traffic-engineering, TE, recompute figure is ~30 s) [27]. |
| 5 | Oversubscribed path (a path offered more traffic than it can carry, by design) | D6 → D3, D4 | Depends on the collective, the communication pattern the job uses: ring collectives "tolerate over-subscription", while all-to-all traffic needs full bisection bandwidth, meaning any half of the fabric can talk to the other half at full rate [27]. So it depends on the workload profile. | No | Structural. Needs a change of capacity or of job placement [27]. |
| 6 | Microburst (a traffic spike lasting microseconds) | D3 (through drops and buffer watermarks) → D4 | Visible only as accumulated drops, watermarks (the highest buffer level reached) or pause time; invisible to average utilization (70% of bursts last tens of µs; the correlation coefficient r = 0.098 at 4-min polling, measured at Facebook) [28] | No | Lasts under a millisecond by definition. The index should respond to the loss that accumulates, not to individual bursts. |
| 7 | Sustained congestion | D3 → D4, D6 | Strong decline in D3 as the fraction of time spent paused and the pressure on buffers persist | No, unless it escalates to CC2 or CC3 | Depends on the operator. | 8 | Packet loss | D3 (→ D1 if it is a black hole, where traffic vanishes without any error being reported) | Strong on lossless classes, the traffic classes configured never to drop. A rise against that reference is evidence of an anomaly, and it lowers D3 only when it coincides with a breach of the reference for throughput, pause or delay. | 10 | PFC storm (pause frames spreading from switch to switch until traffic stalls) | D3 → D1, D6 (capacity is suppressed while ports are paused) | Severe. | 11 | Retransmission spike (a sudden rise in packets that had to be sent again) | D3 | Down. How much it means depends on the transport: with go-back-N, one lost packet forces everything sent after it to be re-sent; with selective retransmission, only the lost packet is [33]. | No, unless it escalates to CC3 | Local ACK timeouts (the sender waiting in vain for an acknowledgement) last milliseconds; they can cause a job to fail [27]. |
| 12 | NIC degradation (a server's network adapter working badly) | D3, D5 (on the NIC's own port), D1 | Localized. | 13 | Partial topology failure (part of the fabric down, the rest working) | D1, D2 → D6 | A gray failure, one that is not clean-cut: throughput may look fine while all-to-all traffic regresses [36] | Yes if CC1 or CC5 | A reboot resumed forwarding by t = 2 min in one case [22]. | 14 | Loss of a redundant path | D2 | D2 falls. The other vitals are often unchanged [36]. | Yes if CC5, judged relative to the failure tolerance the owner declared | Persists until repair. Hidden by design: the redundancy did its job, so nothing else shows. |
| 15 | Routing instability | D1 (through the routing-stability metric, M44) → D6 (flows colliding after being reassigned) | Passing loss and micro-loops, packets briefly circling between switches whose routes disagree [23]; flow collisions after a failure makes ECMP reassign flows to new paths [27] | Yes if it produces CC1 | Recovery under a second is possible; it takes several seconds when the failure is detected only by keepalive messages [23]. |
| 16 | Telemetry blindness (the measurements stop arriving for part of the fabric) | Coverage rather than a vital: the confirmed score (the lower bound) falls, the upper bound rises, and the coverage figure is shown. See SPEC.md §8. | A gap shorter than Δ + G is not blindness, where Δ is the declared sampling interval and G is the grace period allowed for late data (Rulebook parameter T30). Beyond that, the unobserved capacity is counted as if it were in the worst state when the confirmed score is computed. Below the coverage floor, the minimum share of the fabric that must be observed to score at all (Rulebook parameter T29), the result is UNSCORABLE. | Only through bounded gates, and the coupling constraint T39 prevents unobserved capacity *alone* from capping a fabric that is otherwise scorable | Returns when the telemetry returns. Faults that go undetected meanwhile persist: manual localization took >4 h for 86.2% of cases in one Tencent study [37]. |
| 17 | Capacity exhaustion (the traffic needs more bandwidth than the fabric has) | D6 → D3, D4 | Down during the phases of a job in which the accelerators exchange data; LLM (large language model) training bursts reach NIC capacity for seconds [24]. In inference, the transfer of the KV cache (the key-value cache, the working memory a model builds up while reading a prompt) needs bandwidth of, for example, 90 Gbps (gigabits per second) for the OPT-66B model at 10 requests per second [38]. | No, unless it escalates to CC3 | Structural, or driven by demand. Mitigated by job placement or by replicating data [39]. |
| 18 | Slow routing convergence after a failure (the switches take too long to agree on new paths) | D2 (recovery measured against the declared objective, metric M45) → D1, D3 (loss while the routes converge) | Down in D2 when the measured convergence time exceeds the routing convergence time the owner declared in the recovery objective (spec-sheet field S11). One individual draft benchmarks it as FIB (forwarding information base, the table a switch actually forwards from) and route convergence time [40]. | Yes if traffic is black-holed for long enough to raise CC1 | BGP convergence takes from under a second to several seconds, depending on how quickly the failure is detected [23]. Measured in the outcome tests of the conformance check and, where probes allow, on real events. See SPEC.md §3. |
| 19 | Planned maintenance (a leaf or spine switch drained of traffic on purpose, in a window declared in advance) | D2 → D6 | D2 falls by the redundancy the drain removes, exactly as it would for an unplanned loss; D6 may fall if the drained capacity was carrying jobs. The contributing findings are tagged PLANNED. See SPEC.md §9.3. | Yes if CC4 or CC5 would fire, tagged PLANNED | Ends when the drained elements return and are observed healthy.
