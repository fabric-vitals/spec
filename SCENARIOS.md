# Fabric Vitals — Scenarios and scoring-formula comparison (v0.2)

**Status:** Fabric Vitals v0.2 (draft) — a technical proposal for discussion. Not a standard. Rulebook values not yet set.

This file supports [`SPEC.md`](SPEC.md) §6 (aggregation) and §7 (critical conditions). It records:
1. **the scoring-formula comparison** behind the choice of the weakest-vital (minimum) rule: how eight families of aggregation formula treat the masking example;
2. **the failure-scenario table**: for nineteen representative AI-fabric failures and events, which vitals should move, whether a critical condition should cap the Vitals Score, and on what time scale the underlying event recovers.

**Every number in the comparison is ILLUSTRATIVE** unless it cites a source. The scenario table is qualitative except where a source is cited. Parameter IDs (T…) refer to [`RULEBOOK.md`](RULEBOOK.md), spec-sheet fields (S…) to [`SPEC_SHEET.md`](SPEC_SHEET.md), and metric IDs to [`METRICS.md`](METRICS.md).

## 1. Scoring-formula comparison: the masking example

**The masking example.** Four vitals are healthy and one has collapsed:
- test vector: 100, 100, 100, 100, **10** (0–100, higher is healthier);
- comparator: a balanced fabric with all five vitals at 82;
- weights: **ILLUSTRATIVE** equal weights (1/5), because no source supplies weights for AI-fabric health;
- cap margin *m*, override threshold *T* and cap *C*: **ILLUSTRATIVE**.

A formula passes if it does not score the collapsed fabric like the balanced one.

### Readings

- **M.3042's two-stage form (family 8)** is non-compensatory only *within* a vital, across entity types. It scores the masking example's collapse at **82.0**, the same as family 1, because that collapse happens *across* vitals, where M.3042 sums. It therefore **fails the masking example** and cannot be the recommended aggregator without dropping that test. Its inner rule is a useful precedent: the Fabric Vitals within-vital rule already reports the worst entity (SPEC §6), and M.3042 is the first ITU-T text found that aggregates by minimum inside a health index.
- **Only the arithmetic mean**, and anything algebraically equal to it for a cross-vital collapse (weighted impairment subtraction, OWA with orness 0.5, M.3042's two-stage form), **scores the collapsed network the same as a uniformly good one.** That rules it out as the sole aggregator, as the OECD/JRC Handbook's own (21,1,1,1) vs (6,6,6,6) example anticipates [4]. It also means **M.3042's level-1 and level-2 weighted sums do not meet the masking example** as written, even though M.3042 uses min inside some level-3 indexes (family 8) [1].
- **The geometric mean**, the HDI's answer to compensation [5], still returns 63 for a network whose congestion health is 10. Whether 63 is acceptable is a policy judgement, and its floor sensitivity makes the normalization floor a de-facto policy parameter.
- **Minimum, unweighted impairment subtraction and small-margin caps** all make the collapsed vital decide the score. The minimum is the only one of them that needs **no weights and no cap constants**.

## 2. Failure scenarios

For each scenario, the table gives:
- which vitals should change (primary → secondary);
- the direction and magnitude (qualitative unless a source is cited);
- whether a critical condition ([`SPEC.md`](SPEC.md) §7) should cap the Vitals Score;
- the evidence-backed recovery time scale of the underlying event.

The score's own hold and decay times are T17 and T18 (VALUE NOT SET). The scenarios are also the basis of validation experiment E1 ([`VALIDATION.md`](VALIDATION.md)).

| # | Scenario | Dimensions that change (primary → secondary) | Direction and magnitude | Cap? | Recovery behaviour (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Link failure | D1 → D2, D6 | Down. Small if redundant and capacity share is small (losing one T0–T1 link is 3% of node capacity in the paper's hypothetical 800G-plane topology, design arithmetic [22]). Large if single-homed. | **Yes if CC4 or CC5** (where the spec sheet promises the lost redundancy, S4/S10), otherwise no | Transport path bypass takes a few tens of µs (MRC design figure); NIC port remap a few seconds [22]; BGP sub-second to multiple seconds [23]; a crashed job rolls back hours [24]. D2 stays low until repair. |
| 2 | Optic degradation | D5 → D3 (loss), D1 (flaps) | Gradual decline in D5; D3 moves once loss appears. Asymmetric faults may not show as link-down [24]. | No, unless it escalates to CC1, CC3 or CC6 | Persists until the optic is replaced. Flaps last "several seconds" per event [25]. |
| 3 | Elevated BER | D5 → D3 | D5 falls as margin to the IEEE budget shrinks. Uncorrectables raise D3 loss. | **Yes if CC6** | Certifying a BER level needs minimum observation windows (≥30 s for 10⁻¹² on 100GbE, ≥75 s for 10⁻¹³ on 400GbE, error-free at ~95% confidence; windows scale inversely with line rate; modeled, 802.1 TSN-fronthaul contribution) [26]. | 4 | ECMP imbalance | D6 → D3, D4 | D6 falls on flow-hash fabrics (up to >30% training degradation from pinning at Meta [27]). Not applicable to spraying fabrics. | No | Persists until re-hash or traffic engineering (Meta TE recomputes ~30 s) [27]. |
| 5 | Oversubscribed path | D6 → D3, D4 | Depends on collective: ring collectives "tolerate over-subscription", all-to-all needs full bisection [27]. Profile-dependent. | No | Structural. Needs a capacity or placement change [27]. |
| 6 | Microburst | D3 (drops, watermarks) → D4 | Visible only as cumulative drops, watermarks or pause time; invisible to average utilization (70% of bursts last tens of µs; r = 0.098 at 4-min polling, Facebook) [28] | No | Sub-ms by definition. | 8 | Packet loss | D3 (→ D1 if black hole) | Strong on lossless classes. | 9 | ECN increase | D3 (method-only reference) | **Ambiguous by design:** ECN is the congestion-control signal [30]. | 10 | PFC storm | D3 → D1, D6 (capacity suppressed) | Severe. | 11 | Retransmission spike | D3 | Down. The meaning depends on the transport (go-back-N vs selective) [33]. | No, unless it escalates to CC3 | Local ACK timeouts are ms-scale; can cause job failure [27]. |
| 12 | NIC degradation | D3, D5 (NIC port), D1 | Localized. | 13 | Partial topology failure | D1, D2 → D6 | Gray: throughput may look fine while all-to-all regresses [36] | **Yes if CC1 or CC5** | Reboot resumed forwarding by t = 2 min in one case [22]. Redundancy stays reduced until repair [36]. |
| 14 | Loss of redundant path | D2 | D2 falls. Other vitals often unchanged [36]. | **Yes if CC5** (relative to the declared tolerance) | Persistent until repair. Hidden by design. |
| 15 | Routing instability | D1 (routing stability, M44) → D6 (reassignment collisions) | Transient loss and micro-loops [23]; flow collisions after failure-driven ECMP reassignment [27] | **Yes if** it produces CC1 | Sub-second possible; multiple seconds with keepalive-only detection [23]. |

| 17 | Capacity exhaustion | D6 → D3, D4 | Down during communication phases; LLM bursts reach NIC capacity for seconds [24]. | 18 | Slow routing convergence after a failure | D2 (recovery against the objective, M45) → D1, D3 (loss while converging) | Down in D2 when the measured convergence time exceeds the routing convergence time declared in the recovery objective (S11). Benchmarked as FIB and route convergence time in one individual draft [40]. | **Yes if** traffic is black-holed long enough to raise CC1 | BGP convergence sub-second to multiple seconds, depending on failure detection [23]. Measured in conformance outcome tests (SPEC §3) and, where probes allow, on real events. |
| 19 | Planned maintenance (a declared drain of a leaf or spine) | D2 → D6 | D2 falls by the redundancy the drain removes, exactly as for an unplanned loss; D6 may fall if drained capacity is job-bearing. Contributors are tagged PLANNED (SPEC §9.3). | **Yes if CC4 or CC5** would fire, tagged PLANNED | Ends when the drained elements return and are observed healthy. Whether the window counts against availability follows the spec sheet (S12). |
